# Maintain the base and use it in projects

## Review on change

Revisit a note when a dependency changes, a provider deprecates an API, an experiment fails, a project exposes a counterexample or a relevant source is corrected. Choose review intervals based on volatility; do not treat a dated “current” label as proof forever.

## Make corrections traceable

Update the current recommendation in place. Record what changed, why, sources and affected notes in a concise change log. Mark the old decision superseded and link the replacement. Avoid endless appendices that leave contradictory instructions simultaneously active.

## Distill instead of copying

For a new project, select relevant notes, verify changeable claims, compare them to project constraints and record adopted choices in that project's context/ADRs. Do not import a whole company's knowledge base or its mandatory stack.

## Keep private and public material separate

Retain client details in their authorized project. A reusable lesson should remove names, identifiers, secrets, internal URLs and business-sensitive evidence. Link privately where authorized rather than duplicating the raw incident or dataset.

## Evaluate usefulness

Track whether a note helped a real decision, whether its experiment still reproduces and whether an agent can find it. Archive redundant or misleading notes. The quality measure is better decisions with less rework, not the number of Markdown files.
